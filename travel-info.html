Plan With Confidence

Practical Kerala Travel Essentials

Use these as planning guidelines, then check current weather, road notices and provider information before travelling.

Best Time To Visit

  • December to February: generally drier and cooler, with higher demand and busier attractions.
  • March to May: warmer and more humid; hill areas can feel milder than the coast.
  • June to September: monsoon scenery and heavier rain, with possible sea, road and activity disruptions.
  • October and November: greener landscapes with occasional post-monsoon rain.

Conditions differ between coastal, hill and northern districts; no season is weather-guaranteed.

Getting To Kerala

Major gateways include Kochi, Thiruvananthapuram, Kozhikode and Kannur, each served by an airport and useful railway connections nearby.

  • Kochi: practical for central routes, Munnar and Alappuzha.
  • Thiruvananthapuram: useful for Varkala and Kovalam.
  • Kozhikode: a common approach for Wayanad.
  • Kannur: useful for the Malabar coast, Valiyaparamba and Bekal.

Confirm current airline and railway timings with the operator.

Getting Around

  • Private AC car or taxi: easiest for hill roads, families, senior travellers and multi-stop routes.
  • Trains: useful between major coastal cities, but do not reach Munnar or central Thekkady.
  • KSRTC buses: economical for students and confident independent travellers.
  • Auto-rickshaws: practical for short local journeys; agree on the meter or fare.
  • Boats and ferries: useful in Kochi and selected backwater areas; check local operating conditions.

Payments & Connectivity

Indian rupees are used. UPI and cards are common in cities and larger hotels, but some cash remains useful for villages, ferries, small cafés and local services.

Mobile coverage can vary in hills, forests and islands. Malayalam is the main language; English is widely used in tourism and many urban services.

Packing & Health

Pack breathable clothes, a light rain layer, sun protection, mosquito repellent, comfortable shoes and personal medicines. Carry motion-sickness support if advised for winding hill roads.

Choose modest clothing for temples and religious places. Families should keep water, simple snacks and spare clothing accessible on transfer days.

Safety

  • Follow beach flags, lifeguards and local warnings; rough monsoon seas can be dangerous.
  • Keep distance from wildlife and never feed animals.
  • Use registered transport and authorised activity providers.
  • Avoid isolated places late at night and share longer transfer details with your stay.
  • Check weather and road notices before hill or backwater journeys.

Travel Thoughtfully

Responsible Tourism

  • Avoid littering and damaging natural areas.
  • Respect village life and do not block roads for photographs.
  • Support local businesses and authorised guides.
  • Follow temple and religious-site rules.
  • Never feed wildlife or disturb habitats.
